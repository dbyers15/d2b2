When someone asks ChatGPT for a recommendation or types a question into Perplexity, they're not getting a list of ten blue links. They're getting an answer, sourced from somewhere, often without a visible citation trail.

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of making your content the source those systems pull from. It's adjacent to SEO but different in ways that matter if you're trying to be found through AI-powered search.

## How do answer engines work differently from search engines?

Traditional search engines like Google serve results. Answer engines like Perplexity, ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, and Claude serve answers — synthesized from multiple sources, often delivered without requiring the user to click through to a website.

This changes the optimization target. With traditional SEO, the goal is a click. With AEO, the goal is to be the source the engine cites or draws from, whether or not the user ever visits your site. Being cited in an AI answer is the new version of ranking first.

The engines that matter most right now:
- **Google AI Overviews**: appears at the top of Google results for many informational queries
- **Perplexity**: growing fast, especially for research-oriented searches
- **ChatGPT Browse**: used for current information when enabled
- **Claude**: strong in professional and analytical contexts

## How is AEO different from SEO?

Both AEO and SEO care about content quality, page speed, and structured data. But the differences are significant:

| | SEO | AEO |
|---|---|---|
| Primary goal | Ranking position | Citation and answer inclusion |
| Success metric | Clicks and traffic | Mentions and citations |
| Optimization focus | Keywords and backlinks | Clear answers and entity authority |
| User action | Click through to your site | Engine reads your content and synthesizes |

SEO is still important. Traditional search still drives most web traffic and AI systems often use search rankings as a quality signal. But AEO requires additional work that SEO alone doesn't cover. Understanding [how attribution tracks across your marketing channels](/blog/marketing-attribution) is useful context here — AEO changes how users find you, which changes how you should think about measuring that traffic.

## What does AEO actually require?

**Clear, direct answers to specific questions.** AI engines scan for content that directly answers the query. Content structured as "Here is the answer to this question" performs better than content that buries the answer in a long essay. Use question-format H2 and H3 headers. Write the answer in the first paragraph after the heading.

**FAQ and structured schema.** FAQ schema markup (JSON-LD) tells Google's crawlers explicitly that your page contains question-and-answer content. This increases the likelihood of appearing in AI Overviews and featured snippets.

**Entity establishment.** AI engines build a knowledge graph of who and what entities are authoritative. For a consultant or business, this means being consistently described the same way across your website, LinkedIn, industry directories, and mentions in other content. The more coherent your entity footprint, the more likely an AI engine is to treat you as authoritative.

**Authoritative, specific content.** AI engines are trained to avoid content that hedges excessively, repeats itself, or is clearly written for SEO rather than for readers. Write for the person asking the question. Be specific. Include examples. Make claims you can support.

**Citations and backlinks still matter.** AI engines use existing quality signals as inputs. Content that has backlinks from authoritative sources is more likely to be included in AI answers. AEO doesn't replace link building. It supplements it.

## Where should you start with AEO?

For most businesses, AEO starts with three things:

First, write content that answers specific questions your clients are actually asking — not just descriptions of your services, but genuinely useful answers to the questions that lead someone to need those services. The [RevOps explainer](/blog/what-is-revops) is a good example of the format: it answers "what is this and do I need it?" directly before going deeper.

Second, add FAQ schema to your site. This is a small technical implementation that has measurable impact on AI Overview inclusion.

Third, build your entity footprint. Consistent presence on LinkedIn, Google Business Profile, and industry directories creates the multi-source corroboration that AI engines use to verify that you're a real, credible entity.

AEO is still an evolving discipline, but the businesses investing in it now are building a moat as AI-powered search grows.
